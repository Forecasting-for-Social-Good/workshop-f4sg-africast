**AFRICAST 2026 – Day 2: Transformations, decompositions, features and basic forecasting**

This is the Day 2 recording of AFRICAST 2026, a free online course in tidy time series analysis and forecasting with R. It was run by Forecasting for Social Good (F4SG) from 19 to 23 October 2026.

🔍 **Session 1: Transformations, decompositions and features** (Mitchell O'Hara-Wild, Monash University)
How to simplify the patterns in time series, then summarise and compare many series at once using features:
• Stabilising variation with log and Box-Cox transformations
• Per capita and inflation adjustments
• Time series decomposition with STL, and seasonal adjustment
• Decomposing series with multiple seasonal periods
• Worked examples: US retail employment, Australian holiday trips and Victorian electricity demand
• The strength of trend and seasonality as features
• Computing feature statistics across many series with `feasts`
• Dimension reduction of features with principal components
• Using features to find unusual series and outliers

🔮 **Session 2: Basic modelling and forecasting** (Mitchell O'Hara-Wild, Monash University)
The tidy forecasting workflow with `fable`, from specifying a model to producing probabilistic forecasts:
• What a forecast is: forecast distributions and prediction intervals
• What can be forecast, and the factors that affect forecastability
• Benchmark methods: mean, naïve, seasonal naïve and drift
• Specifying and estimating models, and working with `mable` objects
• Producing and visualising forecasts, including bootstrapped futures with `generate()`
• Fitted values and residuals

🌍 **About AFRICAST**
AFRICAST is part of the Forecasting for Social Good (F4SG) initiative. It builds forecasting skills among researchers in Africa so that forecasts can inform planning and resource allocation. The project is led by Bahman Rostami-Tabar (Cardiff University). The instructors are Mitchell O'Hara-Wild, Abiodun Egwuenu, Harsha Halgamuwe Hewage and Mustafa Aslan, and Henry Kissinger Ochieng leads coordination. Learn more at https://www.f4sg.org/
